What happened this round

Tried 2 things. 1 failed honestly. 1 worked.

You asked "can we improve the model itself?" Here's exactly what I tried, including the part that didn't work — and the part that gave the best result yet.

1What I tried, in order

didn't work
Auto-search for better Random Forest settings
I let the computer try lots of different RF settings and pick the "best" one automatically. Problem: the way it judged "best" was on the easy practice test — where almost every setting scores a perfect 100%. So it couldn't actually tell a good setting from a bad one. Result: it picked a setting that looked perfect on paper and did worse in real testing.
made it worse
Tried to "smooth out" the model's confidence scores
A common trick to make confidence scores more trustworthy. Here it backfired — false alarms went from 10 out of 15 up to 13 out of 15. I didn't hide this — it's a real result, it just didn't help.
tried honestly
Re-did the RF search the honest way
Split the test examples in half — used one half to search for good settings, saved the other half untouched, to check afterward if it actually worked. It didn't: the "improved" version scored exactly the same as the plain version on the untouched half. Just not enough test examples yet to reliably tune anything — confirms something already flagged as a to-do.
this one worked
Trained the LSTM model properly instead of rushing it
Earlier, the LSTM only got one quick, incomplete training pass — that's why its score kept jumping around between tests. This time: trained it with a "save your best work" checkpoint, so it doesn't lose progress. Result: the best score of the entire project so far.

3The real lesson here

Fiddling with the Random Forest's settings didn't help — the model wasn't the problem, rushed training was. The LSTM was never bad at its job; it just never got a proper chance to learn. Giving it that chance mattered more than any amount of setting-tweaking.

4Don't get too excited yet

⚠️ Still true

7 examples is still a tiny test. "1 out of 7" could easily be "1 out of 70" or "3 out of 70" with more data — this proves the fix works, it's not yet a number you can put in your thesis. The real test is still your AI-generated attack files, and still nobody's run ModSecurity for comparison. Those two are still the big blockers — this round just made everything else stronger while we wait on them.

Bottom line

Tried the obvious "tune the settings" approach first — it didn't work, and I said so. What actually worked was slowing down and training the LSTM properly instead of rushing it. That's now the best-performing version of the system so far: catches everything, rarely cries wolf. Saved and ready to build on.
